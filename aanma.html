A scan shows only part of the story. The same shadow on a chest X-ray means one thing for a 30-year-old with a cough, and another for a 70-year-old on blood thinners.

One case, step by step

From scan to signed report.

An example case, not patient data. The times show the order of events only.

  1. 09:02

    Scan arrives

    A chest X-ray for a 70-year-old who is short of breath reaches the radiology queue.

  2. 09:02

    History joins the scan

    With the patient’s consent, AANMA opens the linked health record: past conditions, recent labs and current medicines.

  3. 09:03

    The risk shows

    The record says the patient is on blood thinners and has a drug allergy. AANMA raises both as alerts.

  4. 09:03

    The case moves up

    AANMA sets the urgency to “First”. The case goes to the top of the radiologist’s queue.

  5. 09:04

    A draft is ready

    AANMA writes a first draft of the report and suggests the next step: more tests or a referral.

  6. 09:11

    The doctor decides

    The radiologist reads the scan, edits the draft and signs. Only then does the report leave.

Capabilities

What AANMA does for the doctor.

01

Reads the scan with the history

X-ray, CT and MRI images read together with the linked record, lab results and medicines.

02

Sorts cases by urgency

Each case gets an urgency level, so the most serious patient reaches a doctor first.

03

Suggests the next step

What to do next for this patient, such as more tests or a referral to a specialist.

04

Flags allergies and risks

Allergies, current medicines and past conditions show as an alert before the doctor decides.

05

Drafts the report

For each scan, a first draft of the report. The doctor edits it and signs it.

06

Learns from doctors

Doctors review and correct its outputs, and each correction goes back into training.

Use cases

Where AANMA works.

Hospitals

Emergency rooms

A patient’s history sits in files and apps that do not share data. AANMA brings the relevant parts into one view, so the doctor sees allergies, medicines and past conditions before deciding.

Diagnostics

Radiology queues

AANMA reads each scan first and sorts the queue by urgency, so the most serious cases reach a radiologist first, with a draft report to edit and sign.

Platforms

Healthcare platforms

Hospital software, health apps, insurers, PACS vendors and teleradiology networks add AANMA to the products they already sell.

Education

Medical colleges

Residents compare their reads and next steps with AANMA’s, and faculty use it for teaching cases and research.

Brands

Healthcare brands

Device and pharma brands offer AANMA inside their own products, under their own name.

Connect by API

Four steps to add AANMA to your product.

  1. Send the scan and the record

    Your platform sends the image with the patient’s linked record, with consent.

  2. AANMA reads both

    It returns an urgency level, a next step, alerts and a draft report.

  3. Show it on your screens

    The output appears inside your own product, where your doctors already work.

  4. The doctor signs

    Nothing reaches the patient until a doctor has reviewed and signed it.

Questions

Questions about AANMA

Does AANMA replace the doctor?

No. AANMA suggests an urgency level, a next step and alerts. A doctor reviews, edits and signs every output.

Why read the history with the scan?

The right next step depends on the patient. Age, past conditions, lab results and medicines change what a finding on a scan means.

Which scans does AANMA read?

X-ray, CT and MRI.

How does a platform connect to AANMA?

By API. The platform sends the scan and the linked record, and shows AANMA’s output inside its own product.

Where is patient data kept?

In the country of the deployment, under its data protection law. Access needs the patient’s consent and is logged.
